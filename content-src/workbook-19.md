# Part 19 workbook: foundations and interpretations

Interpretations are argued about, not memorized. For each one, be able to say what is real, whether collapse happens, and where it pays a price. Several of these exercises have real numbers in them, because many paradoxes are calculations.

### 19.1 The measurement problem

**Checks**

- [ ] I can explain why linear evolution clashes with single outcomes.

**Exercises**

1. A detector ends up reading "up" for ∣↑⟩ and "down" for ∣↓⟩. Use linearity to find what happens to (∣↑⟩ + ∣↓⟩)/√2.
2. Why does that result conflict with what we see?
3. List the three ways out: add collapse, add hidden variables, or accept all outcomes. Name one interpretation for each.

### 19.1b Deriving a Bell inequality

**Checks**

- [ ] I can derive the CHSH bound from local hidden variables.

**Exercises**

1. Give each of A, A′, B and B′ a preset value of ±1. Show that AB − AB′ + A′B + A′B′ = A(B − B′) + A′(B + B′).
2. Explain why one of the two brackets is always 0 and the other ±2.
3. Conclude that the average is at most 2. Compare this with 2√2 from 18.29.
4. Which assumption fails if nature breaks the bound?
