# Part 0 workbook: orientation and absolute basics

From counting to scientific notation: the exercises start trivially easy and end with estimates about atoms and the universe.

### 0.1 Map of mathematics

**Checks**

- [ ] I can name at least five branches of mathematics.
- [ ] I can say which branches quantum mechanics leans on most.

**Exercises**

1. Name the branch for each problem: (a) counting ways to arrange 5 books, (b) the area under a curve, (c) the chance of rain tomorrow, (d) whether a coffee mug and a donut are "the same shape", (e) solving 3 equations in 3 unknowns.
2. Before Part 3, write one sentence guessing why quantum mechanics needs linear algebra. Keep it and check yourself later.

### 0.2 Map of physics

**Checks**

- [ ] I can place quantum mechanics, relativity and thermodynamics on the map.
- [ ] I can explain what "quantum gravity" is trying to unify.

**Exercises**

1. Name the field of physics behind each: a rainbow, the clock correction in GPS satellites, a fridge, a transistor, a compass needle, a planet's orbit.
2. Which two theories does quantum gravity try to combine?

### 0.3 Map of chemistry

**Checks**

- [ ] I can explain how chemistry sits between physics and biology.

**Exercises**

1. Name the branch of chemistry for: why iron rusts, how a painkiller acts in the body, why salt dissolves in water, designing a better battery.
2. Which physics theory explains why atoms form bonds at all?

### 0.4 Map of quantum physics

**Checks**

- [ ] I can name three things I use every day that rely on quantum mechanics.
- [ ] I can name the main sub-areas of quantum physics from the map.

**Exercises**

1. Before watching, write what you think "quantum" means in one sentence. After watching, rewrite it.
2. Match each technology to its quantum effect: laser, MRI scanner, transistor, the Sun shining. Effects: tunneling, stimulated emission, nuclear spin, energy bands in solids.

### 0.5 The scientific method

**Checks**

- [ ] I can explain what makes a claim falsifiable.
- [ ] I can design a simple experiment that could prove a claim wrong.

**Exercises**

1. Which claims are falsifiable? (a) Invisible, undetectable dragons live in my garage. (b) All swans are white. (c) Every electron has a charge of −1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C.
2. Someone says heavy objects fall faster. Design a test with two balls and a phone camera. What result would prove them wrong?
3. Why do physicists insist that other labs repeat an experiment before trusting it?

### 0.6 Number sets

- [ ] I can explain why every integer is also rational.

**Exercises**

2. Write 0.272727… as a fraction in lowest terms.
3. Is there any number between 0.999… and 1? What does that tell you?
4. Is √−1 in any of these sets? (You will meet it in entry 1.37.)
