# Part 22 workbook: quantum gravity and cosmology

Nobody knows the final answer here, so the exercises lean on what can be calculated: Planck units, black-hole temperatures and entropies, and the famous mismatch of about 120 orders of magnitude in the cosmological constant. Constants: G = 6.674 × 10⁻¹¹, ħ = 1.055 × 10⁻³⁴, c = 2.998 × 10⁸, k_B = 1.381 × 10⁻²³ (SI units), M_☉ = 1.989 × 10³⁰ kg.

### 22.1 Why quantum gravity is hard

**Checks**

- [ ] I can explain why gravity resists the usual methods.

**Exercises**

1. The gravitational coupling between two protons is Gm_p²/(ħc). Compute it and compare with α ≈ 1/137.
2. Why does gravity's coupling grow with energy, unlike QED's? (See 20.23.)
3. Why can't we simply run an experiment at the Planck energy?

**Exercises**

### 22.3 Hawking radiation

**Checks**

- [ ] I can compute Hawking temperatures and lifetimes.

**Exercises**

1. Use T = ħc³/(8πGMk_B) to find T for a one-solar-mass black hole.
2. Compare it with the CMB at 2.725 K. Is the black hole shrinking or growing today?
3. What mass of black hole would be exactly as warm as the CMB? Compare it with the Moon (7.3 × 10²² kg).
4. The lifetime is 5120πG²M³/(ħc⁴). Compute it for one solar mass, in years.
5. Lifetime goes as M³. Roughly what mass evaporates in the universe's age of 13.8 billion years?
